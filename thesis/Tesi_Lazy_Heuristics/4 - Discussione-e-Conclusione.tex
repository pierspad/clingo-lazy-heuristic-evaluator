\section{Discussion and Conclusions}

\subsection{Discussion of the Results}

The work shows that an important part of the cost of declarative heuristics can be moved from grounding to search. In the BSP benchmark, native \lstinline|#heuristic| directives grow very rapidly: at $n=100$, the native variants exceed one million ground heuristic objects. The lazy version keeps two \lstinline|__heuristic| facts and delegates the evaluation of runtime candidates to the propagator.

This result should not be interpreted as a replacement for general lazy grounding. clingo still grounds the base program. The propagator acts only on the heuristic component, namely on the representation and evaluation of rules whose purpose is to guide search. Consequently, reducing the heuristic component does not remove the growth of the BSP rules, but it makes one specific source of growth explicit, measurable, and controllable.

The second central aspect is semantic. The thesis distinguishes clingo-like semantics, where negative bodies and aggregates are evaluated conservatively, from Alpha-style semantics, where non-truth may already make a negative body applicable and aggregates are read with respect to the partial state. This distinction is essential. The \lstinline|la| variant has very strong solving performance, but it does not follow the same semantics as \lstinline|gc|. The \lstinline|lc| variant is more appropriate for measuring the lazy representation against a clingo-like interpretation, even though its search behaviour remains closer to the baseline.

This semantic distinction is independent of ASP correctness. Heuristics do not change the set of answer sets; they only influence how the solver searches for them. The semantic validator therefore compares the visible models of the problem after filtering internal \lstinline|__...| symbols, while the experimental metrics measure how the heuristic representation changes grounding and search. In the same spirit, variants such as \lstinline|la_co| must be interpreted as experiments on the BSP model, not as direct comparisons between heuristic representations alone.

\subsection{Limitations}

The current implementation has several technical and semantic limitations. It supports only targets and bodies that can be indexed through numeric tuples. Arithmetic expressions are limited to addition, subtraction, and multiplication. The available dynamic aggregates are \lstinline|sum|, \lstinline|count|, \lstinline|min|, and \lstinline|max|. The modifiers \lstinline|init| and \lstinline|factor| are recognized but not implemented, because native clingo uses them to update the internal heuristic score, whereas the current propagator interface can only return a decision literal. The tie-break between targets with the same resolved level is deterministic but does not reproduce the full internal score of the solver. Finally, the propagator is registered automatically and sequentially, and the main benchmark is currently concentrated on BSP.

The limitation on \lstinline|init| and \lstinline|factor| is particularly important. Treating them as \lstinline|level| would make the prototype appear more complete, but it would give those modifiers a misleading semantics. The implementation therefore prefers an explicit limitation over an approximation that could invalidate comparisons with native clingo.

\subsection{Implications}

The main implication is that declarative heuristics can be treated as an intermediate layer between modelling and solving. One does not have to choose only between fully grounded \lstinline|#heuristic| directives and procedural heuristics embedded directly in solver code. The \lstinline|__heuristic| representation keeps a declarative surface, while the propagator evaluates it at runtime with access to the partial assignment.

From an engineering perspective, the extension also shows that clingo's propagator and heuristic interfaces are flexible enough to prototype non-trivial search guidance mechanisms. The propagator does not add constraints. Instead, it intervenes at decision time, combining watched literals, incremental aggregate states, per-target modifier resolution, and a global ranking of currently active targets.

\subsection{Future Work}

Several extensions follow naturally from the current prototype. The propagator should become configurable, for example through a command-line option or by activating it only when \lstinline|__heuristic| facts are present. The expression language could be extended with integer division, modulo, comparisons, and a principled treatment of non-numeric symbols through a stable internal mapping. The tie-break policy could be improved by interacting more closely with the solver fallback heuristic, where the interface allows it.

On the evaluation side, the next step is to test equivalence more systematically between native \lstinline|#heuristic| directives and the clingo-like lazy form, and to move beyond BSP. Configuration, scheduling, and packing benchmarks are natural candidates, because dynamic heuristics are often meaningful in those domains. Another promising direction is a richer converter that turns more complex native directives into lazy forms, with clearer diagnostics and stronger guarantees about the transformations it applies.

\subsection{Conclusions}

This thesis presented an extension of clingo for the lazy evaluation of declarative heuristics. The system introduces a syntax based on \lstinline|__heuristic| facts, a dedicated parser, runtime structures for heuristic candidates, incremental dynamic aggregates, and a clingo-like treatment of local priority, level, and sign.

The BSP evaluation shows that the ground heuristic component can be reduced drastically: from more than one million native directives to two lazy facts at $n=100$. The main benefit is not only quantitative. The work separates the representation cost of the heuristic from the grounding cost of the base program, and it makes explicit the semantic difference between Alpha-style and clingo-like evaluation.

The final result is a working, documented, and measurable prototype. It does not solve the general grounding bottleneck by itself, but it provides a concrete path for bringing dynamic declarative heuristics into clingo without always paying the cost of their full ground materialization.
