\section{Results}

\subsection{Functional Validation}

The system was validated by keeping three levels separate. The first level concerns the ASP program itself: the answer sets, the \mintinline{text}|SAT|/\mintinline{text}|UNSAT| result, and the visible solutions must remain consistent across the compared encodings and across the two backends. The second level concerns the heuristic translation: targets, bodies, weights, priorities, modifiers, and aggregate bindings must be preserved when a native \mintinline{text}|#heuristic| directive is rewritten as a lazy fact, including the weight--priority flattening where the clingo reading of priorities applies. The third level concerns the effect on search, measured through choices, conflicts, solving time, memory, and the size of the ground heuristic component.

Heuristics do not change the semantics of the ASP program: they guide search, but they do not add or remove answer sets. The equivalence harness of Section~2 verifies this property exhaustively on BSP, by enumerating all models of every variant on both backends and comparing the collections after projection on \mintinline{text}|b/1| and \mintinline{text}|c/1|, and by SAT/UNSAT agreement on PUP, where exhaustive enumeration is intractable. The same harness enforces the anti-fallback guards for the Prolog backend (\mintinline{text}|decide_calls > 0| for every lazy Prolog run, and equal choice counts between native and Prolog \mintinline{text}|la| on the control instance). On the native side, the C++ test suite isolates the correctness of the heuristic machinery from the benchmarks: it covers incremental aggregate states, empty and unsatisfiable corner cases, explicit body and target bindings, filtered aggregates, the local-versus-global interpretation of priorities under the two semantics, and the parser's rejection of malformed syntax.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

The campaign reported in this section was executed on an HPC cluster under SLURM, with one job per (system, setting, instance) triple orchestrated by \mintinline{text}|benchmark-tool|. Every run was limited by \mintinline{text}|runlim| to 300 seconds of real time and 8~GiB of resident memory, on 4 dedicated cores. Both backends were compiled on the compute nodes against the same toolchain, and the Prolog backend embeds a modern SWI-Prolog (10.0.2) built in user space for the same environment. The dataset covers the three families with their canonical instance ranges: BSP with $n = 10$ to $120$ in steps of $10$; PUP with the \mintinline{text}|double-$N$| instances, $N = 20$ to $200$ in steps of $20$ communication units; HRP with the reconfiguration instances \mintinline{text}|house-$N$|, $N = 2$ to $20$ persons in steps of $2$. All five shared settings run on all families; the three methodological extras (\mintinline{text}|ga_weak|, \mintinline{text}|la_aux|, \mintinline{text}|la_co|) are BSP-specific. The grand total is 392 runs (192 for BSP, 100 each for PUP and HRP), none of which ended in error: every unsolved run is an explicit timeout (\emph{T}) or memout (\emph{M}).

The figures of this chapter follow the same separation. Every comparative plot shows the reference encoding and the four variants under study, \mintinline{text}|gc_noheur|, \mintinline{text}|gc|, \mintinline{text}|ga|, \mintinline{text}|la|, and \mintinline{text}|lc|; the three exploratory settings are kept out of these plots on purpose, because each of them answers a different, narrower question, and appear only in the dedicated analyses of Sections~\ref{sec:ga-weak}, \ref{sec:aux-form}, and~\ref{sec:constr-form}, in figures restricted to the variants that each comparison actually needs.

Two properties anchor the validity of the numbers. First, within each system the ground variants (\mintinline{text}|g*|) execute identical binaries on identical ground programs, and clasp's search is deterministic at fixed configuration: choice and conflict counts are therefore exact structural measures, not noisy samples, and they reproduce bit-for-bit the counts observed in the preliminary local campaign (for example, \mintinline{text}|gc| at $n=100$ performs exactly $32{,}514$ choices on both machines). Second, the anti-fallback guard confirmed \mintinline{text}|decide_calls > 0| for every solved lazy Prolog run, so no lazy measurement silently degenerated to the baseline.

Three headline facts are already visible at this granularity and are unpacked in the following subsections. The Alpha-like lazy variant \mintinline{text}|la| is the most robust setting of the whole matrix: it is the only heuristic variant that never times out, on any family, on either backend. Finally, the clingo-like lazy variant \mintinline{text}|lc| exhibits two distinct failure modes, inertness on BSP and active misguidance on HRP, that the per-decision instrumentation makes directly measurable.

\subsection{Reduction of the Ground Heuristic Component}

The central claim of the thesis concerns \emph{where} memory is spent, and the direct evidence is the number of ground heuristic objects as a function of $n$. The mechanism is readable in the encoding itself. The standard directive

\begin{minted}{prolog}
#heuristic b(X) : x(X), not c(X), S = #sum{Y : c(Y)},
    W = ((n+1)*S)+X. [W, true]
\end{minted}

carries the aggregate value $S$ \emph{as a variable inside the weight}. Since the atoms \mintinline{text}|c(Y)| are choice atoms, the grounder cannot know the sum at grounding time and must materialize one directive for every reachable pair $(X,S)$. With $X \in \{1,\ldots,n\}$ and $S$ ranging over the subset sums of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, which is every integer from $0$ to $n(n+1)/2$ because $1$ belongs to the domain, the count is
\[
2 \cdot n \cdot \Big(\tfrac{n(n+1)}{2} + 1\Big) \;=\; \Theta(n^3),
\]
where the factor $2$ accounts for the two symmetric rules for \mintinline{text}|b| and \mintinline{text}|c|. The formula is verifiable exactly on the measured data: for $n=10$ it yields $2 \cdot 10 \cdot 56 = 1{,}120$, which coincides with the measured \mintinline{text}|ground_heuristics| value for \mintinline{text}|gc|. The explicit unrolling of \mintinline{text}|ga| materializes the same $(X,S)$ product by construction. The lazy variants remove this enumeration at the root: the aggregate is evaluated at solving time on the current partial assignment, and the materialized heuristics remain constant, two facts, independent of $n$.

This comparison must be read for what it is. For the ground variants the count is faithful: one line is one materialized object. For the lazy variants the two counted facts are \emph{templates} that expand at runtime into one candidate per ground target ($n$ candidates per rule for BSP); those candidates never pass through the grounder and therefore appear in no line count. The single-figure comparison \enquote{$2$ facts versus $\Theta(n^3)$ directives} is thus the proof of a saving in \emph{grounding space}, not of a saving in total work: the work moved to runtime is real and is measured separately in Section~\ref{sec:per-decision-cost}.

Three observations follow from Table~\ref{tab:bsp-n120}. First, \mintinline{text}|la| turns search into a formality, 120 choices and zero conflicts, so its total time is the grounding time of the base program: it is the fastest same-encoding variant at every size from $n=60$ upwards and solves the whole range, whereas the ground directives push \mintinline{text}|gc| over the limit at $n=120$ (the run was killed with at least 130 seconds already spent in solving on top of a grounding component inflated to 169 seconds).

Second, \mintinline{text}|ga| is the surprise of the full campaign, and a negative one. Its static unrolling was designed as the faithful ground simulation of the Alpha reading, and Section~\ref{tab:heur-growth} shows it pays the same $\Theta(n^3)$ grounding as \mintinline{text}|gc|; but unlike \mintinline{text}|gc|, whose negative guards deactivate directives as atoms become established, the unrolled directives of \mintinline{text}|ga| lack those guards by design, so the Domain heuristic is flooded with simultaneously applicable modifications. The search degenerates: at $n=60$, \mintinline{text}|ga| needs $150$ seconds against $8.5$ of the baseline with $1.6$ million choices, and from $n=70$ it never finishes within the limit (at $n=120$ it was killed at $238{,}397$ choices and counting). The faithful simulation is thus \emph{doubly} punished, once in the grounder and once in the solver: an instructive datum for anyone hoping to emulate dynamic heuristics in a ground-and-solve pipeline.

Third, Table~\ref{tab:bsp-n120} contains two rows that the figures of this section deliberately omit. \mintinline{text}|ga_weak|, the cheap approximation of the Alpha reading, stays close to the baseline and is compared against the faithful \mintinline{text}|ga| in Section~\ref{sec:ga-weak}; \mintinline{text}|la_co|, which changes the problem model itself, is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:constr-form}.

\subsection{BSP: the Two Semantics Under the Microscope}
\label{sec:bsp-semantics}

Search statistics separate the two semantics sharply, and the full campaign adds an instrumentation-level proof of \emph{why}. The \mintinline{text}|la| variant makes exactly $n$ choices with zero conflicts at every size: the Alpha reading makes the greedy balance heuristic total, one direct decision per element, which is the known optimal strategy for this problem. The \mintinline{text}|lc| variant is not merely \enquote{close to the baseline}: it is \emph{search-identical} to it. At every size, its choice and conflict counts coincide with \mintinline{text}|gc_noheur| to the last unit ($78{,}565$ and $49{,}993$ at $n=120$).

    \hfill

    \caption{BSP, native backend: choices and conflicts. \texttt{la} lies on the horizontal axis; \texttt{lc} coincides with the baseline; \texttt{ga} leaves the chart after $n=60$.}
    \label{fig:bsp-search}

The Prolog instrumentation explains the coincidence. On BSP the \mintinline{text}|lc| rule guards the aggregate source with the clingo reading: the sum over \mintinline{text}|c/1| becomes usable only when every \mintinline{text}|c| atom is determined, and \mintinline{text}|clingo_not(c(X))| additionally requires established falsity. During search these conditions are essentially never satisfied at decision time: the backend reports an average of \emph{zero} applicable candidates per decision (\mintinline{text}|avg_candidates_per_decide| $= 0.0$ over $78{,}565$ decide calls at $n=120$). Every decide call returns nothing, the solver falls back to its default heuristic, and the trajectory reproduces the baseline exactly. The clingo-like lazy variant on BSP is therefore \emph{inert by semantics}: it costs the full decide-loop machinery and buys nothing, which is precisely what the design predicts for a heuristic whose guards wait for information that only exists at the bottom of the search tree.

\subsection{BSP: Rules, Variables, and Memory}

The compression of the heuristic component is visible in the size of the propositional instance. At $n=100$, \mintinline{text}|gc| is fifty times larger in solver variables than every other variant, because its heuristic directives materialize the negative body and the aggregate bound for each $(X,S)$ pair. Notably, \mintinline{text}|ga| does \emph{not} inflate the variable count ($20{,}402$, in line with the baseline): its unrolled directives reuse the same aggregate structures, and its cost shows up as grounding time and unguarded activations instead. The two ground variants thus pay the same combinatorial product in two different currencies.

\subsection{The Price of Laziness: Per-Decision Cost}
\label{sec:per-decision-cost}

Intellectual honesty requires stating that the lazy approach does not \emph{eliminate} the cost of the combinatorial explosion: it \emph{moves} it. What ground-and-solve pays once, in memory, at grounding time, the lazy backend pays repeatedly, in time, at every decision. The full campaign turns this from a declared principle into measured numbers, through the instrumentation of the Prolog backend (the native backend implements the same decision loop without per-phase timers, so the Prolog figures are an upper bound for it).

The practical reading is that \emph{lazy heuristics are cheapest exactly when they work}: effective guidance keeps the decision count, and therefore the total overhead, small.

\subsection{Two Ground Simulations of Alpha: \texttt{ga} versus \texttt{ga\_weak}}
\label{sec:ga-weak}

The three subsections that follow analyze the exploratory settings. They are not alternative candidates in the main comparison: each one isolates a single design decision, and its figures include only the variants that decision touches.

The first decision is how faithfully the Alpha reading can be forced into a ground-and-solve pipeline. \mintinline{text}|ga| is the faithful simulation and pays for both halves of it: it drops the negative literals so that established truth does not deactivate the directives, \emph{and} it statically unrolls the aggregate, replacing the equality binding of $S$ with the monotone bound $S \le \#\mathtt{sum}\{Y : c(Y)\}$ over every candidate value, so that clasp's max-weight selection reconstructs the current value of the growing sum at solving time (Section~2). \mintinline{text}|ga_weak| keeps only the first half: it treats the negation as Alpha does and leaves the aggregate untouched, which means the grounder evaluates the sum once, on the empty assignment, freezing $S=0$ and degrading the heuristic to a static ranking of the elements by $X$.

Figure~\ref{fig:gaweak} shows what each half costs. The unrolled \mintinline{text}|ga| explodes exactly where the unguarded activations flood the Domain heuristic: at $n=60$ it needs $1.6$ million choices and $142$ seconds of solving against $8.5$ seconds of the baseline, and from $n=70$ it never finishes, the double punishment already diagnosed in Section~\ref{sec:bsp-runtime}. \mintinline{text}|ga_weak| sits at the opposite end: structurally it is indistinguishable from the baseline (same rules, constraints, and variables, $20{,}406$ versus $20{,}300$ at $n=100$, because nothing is unrolled), and behaviourally it stays close to it over the whole range ($232.5$ versus $204.3$ seconds at $n=120$, with somewhat noisier search: $121{,}159$ choices against $78{,}565$). It solves all twelve instances precisely because it renounces the dynamic part of the heuristic; the frozen $S=0$ ranking neither guides nor obstructs.

Read together, the two simulations bracket the lazy propagator from both sides and confirm that the benefit measured for \mintinline{text}|la| comes from the \emph{dynamic} aggregate, not from the mere presence of weights or from the Alpha activation rule alone. The half of the Alpha reading that is cheap to ground (\mintinline{text}|ga_weak|) is behaviourally inert, and the half that reproduces the guidance (\mintinline{text}|ga|) is combinatorially unaffordable; only the runtime evaluation of the aggregate obtains the guidance without the unrolling, which is the design point the lazy propagator occupies.

\subsection{Direct and Auxiliary Forms}
\label{sec:aux-form}

The \mintinline{text}|la_aux| variant routes the lazy heuristic through auxiliary predicates that materialize the heuristic body, including the aggregate value, before the propagator is used. This experiment separates two effects that are otherwise easy to confuse: reducing the number of ground heuristic directives, and avoiding the materialization of the heuristic body itself. \mintinline{text}|la_aux| keeps only two \mintinline{text}|\_\_heuristic| facts, but its auxiliaries \mintinline{text}|h_b(X,S)| and \mintinline{text}|h_c(X,S)| reintroduce the $(X,S)$ product in the base program, and the full campaign shows the consequences from three angles at once. In the grounder, the auxiliary atoms inflate the instance ($145{,}600$ solver atoms at $n=50$ against $10{,}000$ for \mintinline{text}|la|). In the propagator, every $(X,S)$ atom becomes a runtime candidate, so the per-decision scan grows with $n^2$: the native backend times out from $n=70$, and at $n=120$ it exhausts the 8~GiB budget during initialization, before the first choice. On the Prolog side the same candidate flood must additionally cross the synchronization bridge, and the variant already fails at $n=30$ ($3.1$ seconds of sync for 573 decisions at $n=20$, against 3 \emph{milliseconds} for \mintinline{text}|la|). The lesson is clear: if the heuristic body is first materialized into a large auxiliary relation, the intended benefit of laziness is lost on every axis at once. The direct lazy form is the meaningful one.

\subsection{Effect of the Constraint Formulation}
\label{sec:constr-form}

The \mintinline{text}|la_co| variant changes not only the heuristic representation but also the BSP constraint itself, replacing the quadratic difference check over \mintinline{text}|sumB|/\mintinline{text}|sumC| with a single interval constraint over one sum, whose bounds are compile-time constants. The effect is dramatic and worth stating exactly: at $n=120$ the propositional instance drops from $52{,}759{,}256$ rules to $366$, and the total time from $204.3$ seconds (baseline) or $160.9$ seconds (\mintinline{text}|la|) to $0.005$ seconds, with the same 117 guided choices and zero conflicts, on both backends (the Prolog run measures $0.096$ seconds, the difference being the fixed engine startup). This measurement completes the diagnosis of Section~\ref{sec:bsp-runtime}: once the lazy heuristic has removed the search cost, the \emph{entire} residual cost of BSP is the ground materialization of the balance check, and a formulation that avoids it removes the last $\Theta$-large term. A comparison between \mintinline{text}|la_co| and \mintinline{text}|la| therefore measures the interaction between lazy heuristics and problem modelling, not the isolated effect of the heuristic representation, and it is reported as a modelling experiment: its role is to show what the combination \enquote{dynamic heuristic + propagation-friendly model} can reach when neither component pays a combinatorial ground price.

\subsection{HRP: Semantics Without Aggregates}
\label{sec:hrp}

HRP is the negation-heavy benchmark: its five-level Romero heuristic contains no aggregates, so the \mintinline{text}|la|/\mintinline{text}|lc| comparison isolates the negation semantics alone. At the benchmark sizes the problem is easy for clasp, every variant except \mintinline{text}|lc| solves every instance in under half a second on the native backend, which makes HRP useless for frontier claims but ideal as a controlled experiment on guidance quality and per-decision cost.

The Alpha reading behaves exactly as designed: \mintinline{text}|la| tracks \mintinline{text}|ga| decision for decision (86 versus 88 choices at $N=14$, zero conflicts for both), confirming on a second problem class that the lazy propagator reproduces the intended Alpha guidance. The clingo reading, by contrast, is \emph{actively toxic} on this heuristic: requiring established falsity on guards such as \mintinline{text}|not fullCabinet(C)| delays every level of the policy until the corresponding closure information exists, and the surviving low-level modifications steer the solver into a pathological region. Native \mintinline{text}|lc| needs $66{,}717$ choices and $32{,}353$ conflicts at $N=14$ (a thousand times the \mintinline{text}|la| count) and times out from $N=16$; the failure is identical on both backends, which certifies it as a property of the semantics, not of any evaluation engine. Where BSP showed the clingo reading as inert, HRP shows it as misleading: the two failure modes of \enquote{late} information are both on record.

The Prolog backend adds the cost dimension. The Romero rules enumerate every applicable (cabinet, thing) and (room, cabinet) pair, more than a thousand candidates per decision at $N=20$, so even the well-guided \mintinline{text}|la| pays $8.5$ milliseconds per consultation, and the misguided \mintinline{text}|lc| multiplies $3.3$ milliseconds by $73{,}143$ consultations: $242$ of its $268$ seconds at $N=14$ are spent inside \mintinline{text}|aggregate_all|-free but candidate-rich Prolog queries, anticipating by two sizes the timeout that search alone would cause. The quantitative gap between the backends on \mintinline{text}|lc|, $1.75$ versus $268$ seconds at $N=14$, is the largest in the whole campaign and is entirely attributable to the per-decision machinery, since the search trajectories coincide.

\subsection{Native versus Prolog: Summary}
\label{sec:backend-summary}

Across the full campaign the two backends never disagree on \emph{what} is solved except where the per-decision overhead itself is the binding constraint (\mintinline{text}|lc| on PUP, $N=100\to40$; \mintinline{text}|la_aux| on BSP, $n=60\to20$; \mintinline{text}|lc| on HRP reaches the same frontier but two orders of magnitude slower). For every ground variant the two systems are byte-identical encodings on the same solver, and their times agree within measurement noise, which doubles as an end-to-end sanity check of the whole pipeline. For the lazy variants the native backend is uniformly at least as fast, with the gap governed by the law of Section~\ref{sec:per-decision-cost}; the Prolog backend remains the more expressive and the more observable one (its instrumentation produced every per-decision number in this chapter), which is the trade-off it was designed to embody.

\subsection{Summary of Results}

The full campaign supports seven conclusions. First, the lazy representation reduces the ground heuristic component from $\Theta(n^3)$ directives to two constant facts on BSP, and the saving is confirmed on real memory wherever the heuristic grounding is a significant share of the footprint, culminating in PUP where it converts directly into a doubled instance frontier (80 $\to$ 160) under an 8~GiB budget. Second, the total runtime does not become constant, because the base program is still fully grounded: on BSP the grounding of the domain encoding dominates every same-encoding variant, and on PUP it is what ultimately stops even \mintinline{text}|la|; the lazy mechanism isolates the heuristic from the growth of the instance, no more and no less. Third, the semantics axis is not a detail: the Alpha reading turns the BSP heuristic into a perfect greedy strategy ($n$ choices, zero conflicts) and reproduces the intended Romero policy on HRP, while the clingo reading is inert on BSP (search-identical to the baseline, with zero applicable candidates per decision) and actively harmful on HRP; representation and semantics must never be conflated. Fourth, the faithful \emph{ground} simulation of the Alpha reading (\mintinline{text}|ga|) is doubly punished at scale, in the grounder by the unrolling and in the solver by the unguarded activations, which makes the lazy propagator not merely cheaper but the only practical carrier of that semantics inside clingo. Fifth, laziness is a space--time trade-off whose runtime side is now measured, not merely declared: the overhead factorizes as decide calls times per-consultation cost, it ranges from unmeasurable (BSP \mintinline{text}|la|) to dominant (HRP \mintinline{text}|lc|), and it is smallest exactly when the heuristic guides well. Sixth, the three exploratory controls delimit the approach from every side: \mintinline{text}|ga_weak| shows that the Alpha activation rule without the dynamic aggregate is behaviourally inert, \mintinline{text}|la_aux| shows that materializing the heuristic body forfeits the benefit entirely, and \mintinline{text}|la_co| shows that pairing the lazy heuristic with a propagation-friendly model eliminates the residual ground cost and solves $n=120$ in milliseconds. Seventh, the two backends agree on every scientific conclusion while differing exactly where their engineering differs, per-decision cost and observability, so the choice between them is a genuine trade-off rather than a correctness question.
