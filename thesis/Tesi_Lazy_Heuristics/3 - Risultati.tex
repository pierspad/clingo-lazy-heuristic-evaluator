\section{Results}

\subsection{Functional Validation}

The system was validated by keeping three levels separate. The first level concerns the ASP program itself: the relevant answer sets, the \mintinline{text}|SAT|/\mintinline{text}|UNSAT| result, and the visible solutions must remain consistent across the compared encodings. The second level concerns the heuristic transformation: targets, bodies, weights, priorities, modifiers, aggregate variables, and target variables must be preserved when a native \mintinline{text}|#heuristic| directive is rewritten as \mintinline{text}|__heuristic(...)| facts. The third level concerns the effect on search, measured through choices, conflicts, solving time, grounding estimates, and the number of ground heuristic objects.

Heuristics do not change the semantics of the ASP program: they guide search, but they do not add or remove answer sets. For this reason, semantic validation compares models after filtering out the internal prototype symbols that start with \mintinline{text}|__|, unless a complete comparison is explicitly requested with \mintinline{text}|--keep-internal|. This behaviour is implemented in \path{test_folder/tools/validate_lazy_semantics.py}. Performance metrics are treated separately, because they measure the impact of the heuristic representation on grounding and search rather than on the set of solutions.

The validation suite includes C++ tests in \path{clingo-modified/libclingo/tests/heuristic_propagator.cc}, corner-case instances in \path{test_folder/instances/corner_cases}, and the BSP benchmark. The C++ tests cover incremental aggregates, empty domains, unsatisfiable instances, \mintinline{text}|self| as the first target argument, explicit target and body bindings, target-specific aggregate filters, local priority resolution, and syntax errors such as duplicated variables or negative indices. These tests are important because they isolate the correctness of the heuristic machinery from the size and modelling details of the benchmark.

\subsection{Experimental Dataset}

\subsection{Reduction of the Ground Heuristic Component}

The clearest result concerns the size of the heuristic component in the grounded program. In the native \mintinline{text}|gc| and \mintinline{text}|ga| variants, the number of ground \mintinline{text}|#heuristic| directives grows rapidly with $n$. In the direct lazy variants \mintinline{text}|la| and \mintinline{text}|lc|, the grounded program keeps two \mintinline{text}|__heuristic| facts: one for target predicate \mintinline{text}|b| and one for target predicate \mintinline{text}|c|.

This is the strongest evidence that the representation itself is effective. The reduction does not mean that the whole ground program becomes constant: the BSP encoding still contains rules and constraints whose size grows with $n$. What becomes compact is the heuristic part, which is no longer expanded into a large family of ground directives and is instead evaluated by the propagator at runtime.

Figure \ref{fig:total-time} reports the total runtime. The decomposition of the measurements shows that, for larger values of $n$, the part not attributed to solving dominates the total cost. This is consistent with the BSP encoding used in the benchmark: even without native heuristic directives, the base program generates millions of ground rules.

\subsection{Choices, Conflicts, and Restarts}

Search statistics reveal a sharp distinction between Alpha-style and clingo-like semantics. The \mintinline{text}|la| variant makes exactly $n$ choices in the measured instances; at $n=100$ it makes 100 choices and no conflicts. The clingo-like variants follow a profile closer to the no-heuristic baseline, even when their ground heuristic representation is smaller.

\subsection{Randomized Search Sensitivity}

The reduced randomized benchmark complements the main BSP run by focusing on the stability of the solving phase. It uses three seeds and two settings: \mintinline{text}|seed_only|, where only \mintinline{text}|--seed| changes, and \mintinline{text}|rand_freq_0_01|, where clingo also receives \mintinline{text}|--rand-freq=0.01|. The relevant output is not the size of the grounded program, but the variation of \mintinline{text}|total_s|, \mintinline{text}|solving_s|, \mintinline{text}|choices|, and \mintinline{text}|conflicts| across seeds.

This experiment answers a specific methodological question: whether different solver trajectories can explain part of the observed runtime behaviour. If the seed-only setting gives identical or nearly identical curves, then the default configuration is stable for the selected instances. If the mildly randomized setting separates the curves by seed, especially in choices and conflicts, then the variability is not just wall-clock noise but a genuine change in the search path. Conversely, grounding-related values such as \mintinline{text}|rules| and \mintinline{text}|ground_lines| should remain stable for the same variant, $n$, and setting; changes there would indicate a measurement problem rather than search sensitivity.

\subsection{Direct and Auxiliary Forms}

The \mintinline{text}|la_aux| variant uses auxiliary predicates to materialize the heuristic body before applying the lazy propagator. This experiment separates two effects that are otherwise easy to confuse: reducing the number of ground \mintinline{text}|#heuristic| directives, and avoiding the materialization of the heuristic body itself.

\mintinline{text}|la_aux| keeps only two \mintinline{text}|__heuristic| facts, but it introduces \mintinline{text}|h_b| and \mintinline{text}|h_c|. The directive component is small, while the auxiliary body can become large. This explains why the variant times out already at $n=60$ in the considered run. The direct lazy form is therefore the most interesting one for this benchmark: if the heuristic body is first materialized into a large auxiliary predicate, part of the intended benefit is lost.

\subsection{Effect of the Constraint Formulation}

The \mintinline{text}|la_co| variant must be read separately from the direct comparison among heuristic representations. It changes not only the representation of the heuristic directive, but also the BSP constraint formulation by using a more propagation-friendly interval constraint. A comparison between \mintinline{text}|la_co| and \mintinline{text}|la| therefore measures the interaction between lazy heuristics and problem modelling, not the isolated effect of the lazy representation.

For this reason, \mintinline{text}|la_co| is useful as an experiment on modelling choices, but it is not used as a direct baseline for claiming that a lazy heuristic is faster than an equivalent ground-and-solve heuristic.

\subsection{Summary of Results}

The experimental results support five conclusions. The lazy representation reduces the ground heuristic component from hundreds of thousands or millions of directives to two descriptive facts in BSP. The total runtime does not become constant because the base program is still grounded by clingo. Alpha-style semantics can guide search very aggressively, but it does not coincide with clingo-like semantics. Auxiliary forms are useful for expressing more general mappings, yet they may reintroduce the grounding cost that direct lazy evaluation tries to avoid. Finally, variants that change the BSP constraint formulation, such as \mintinline{text}|la_co|, must be analysed as modelling experiments rather than direct comparisons among heuristic encodings.
